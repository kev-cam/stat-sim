Wiring does the rotate.
QAL takes half of it back.

The insight was that a rotate costs zero gates but real wire capacitance, and that wire is the one capacitance an adiabatic rail recovers instead of dissipating. Measured on the committed per-bank-tank chain with a PDK-extracted 32-bit-rotate load: true where the wire sits on the resonant node, and worth almost nothing where a rotate actually lives.

Cheaper than CMOS on the same capacitance at the same measured swing. Net cost 0.400–0.994× the wire's own stored energy per cycle, against CMOS's 2.000.

Wire on signal nodes — a real rotate 1.00–1.59×

Behind the driving cell's pull-up, outside the LC loop. With the tank re-sized to hold the rail it is 1.003× — break-even to three digits.

Signal nodes at the PDK's XOR2 bit pitch 0.749×

QAL loses. And this row is the only one that stops computing: fcrit S3 27/32, failing inside the noise budget at +3.263 mV.

Every ratio is a difference between two decks identical apart from one capacitor, so the cells' own load, the gate drives, the switch loss and the baseline tank loss cancel exactly. The CMOS comparator carries the identical capacitance on the identical cells and its supply is set to the QAL variant's own measured swing peak; it reproduces n·Cw·V² to 0.4–0.6 %.

b
the wire model

0.15 fF/µm was assumed. It is now bracketed by the PDK.

Min-width Metal2, read straight out of sg13g2_tech.lef — the same RC OpenROAD and librelane use for this process. CPERSQDIST = 1.81e-05 pF/µm² and EDGECAPACITANCE = 4.47e-05 pF/µm per edge give an isolated wire over the plane below at 0.0930 fF/µm. That is measured from the PDK: the numbers are read, not modelled.

The LEF model carries no same-layer coupling, and a rotate bundle is a bus. The coupling term is derived from the ITF geometry (thickness 0.450 µm, spacing 0.210 µm, εr = 4.1) by the Sakurai–Tamaru closed form, whose isolated prediction independently reproduces the LEF figure to 84 % — which is what makes it trustworthy for the part the LEF omits.

How long a rotate wire is

Bit i goes to bit (i+r) mod W, so W−r bits travel r pitches and r bits travel W−r (the wrap). Mean length is 2r(W−r)/W bit pitches — 16.0 / 15.0 / 12.0 / 10.94 for ChaCha20's r = 16 / 12 / 8 / 7, mean 13.48. At the brief's own geometry (32 bits, r = 16, 1 µm bit pitch) that is 16 µm per bit and 2.400 fF per rotated bit; 32 bits × 16 µm × 0.15 fF/µm = 76.8 fF against the brief's "~75 fF". Reproduced.

Correction 1 — the wire is a smaller share than supposed 20–33 %

Of the committed bank's own measured 35.979 fF, at the nominal 0.15 fF/µm. The brief's "a third to a half" holds only at the top of the PDK band (29–49 % at 0.2181 fF/µm).

Correction 2 — the 64-cell bank figure 287.8 fF

Not 128 fF. The committed 8-cell bank measures 35.979 fF → 4.497 fF/cell → 64 cells is 287.8 fF for inverters, and an XOR2 is larger. A 2.25× understatement, and it works against the insight.

The energy split, and how much wire charge actually comes back

Signal wire · matched tank −53.9 %

Incremental return. The tanks push 24.793 fJ more into the rails and get 13.369 fJ less back than they did without the wire. Net cost 39.519 fJ to store 19.809 fJ.

Per-bank recycle falls 25.41 / 17.12 / 19.11 / 18.81 % → 19.62 / 9.42 / 13.73 / 11.87 %. The wire does not merely fail to recover — it degrades the recovery of the bank's own charge.

Rail wire · matched tank +49.9 %

Incremental return. 40.646 fJ in, 20.295 fJ back. Net cost 25.951 fJ to store 26.106 fJ — one-to-one.

Per-bank recycle rises 25.41 / 17.12 / 19.11 / 18.81 % → 28.32 / 22.26 / 22.37 / 23.94 %. The only term in this campaign where adding capacitance improves the recycling.

This refutes the mechanism I pre-registered

I predicted the pull-up pMOS would stop conducting at |Vtp| = 0.4403 V and cap recovery at 80.6–82.4 %. Measured, the output-node end voltages are 0.7587 / 0.7871 / 0.8000 / 0.8000 V and the rail end voltages are 0.7587 / 0.7871 / 0.8000 / 0.8000 V — equal to four decimals. The pMOS conducts all the way down and the threshold never binds. The wire's recovery is capped by the rail's own recovery: the rail stops at 0.76–0.80 V out of a 1.21 V peak, because the committed return stroke removes only about 60 % of the rail's stored charge.

The loop quality factor was the wrong model, and I used it in my own pre-registration

I predicted 5–40× for the rail wire from (2/π)·√(L/C_eff)/R = 43.11×. That reference assumes the only loss is the 10 Ω series resistance — and the measured series-R loss is 0.82–1.06 fJ per hop, against roughly 43 fJ dissipated inside the bank on the way in (68.5 fJ pushed into the rail for 25.3 fJ of storage) and 6.7–8.6 fJ in the switch block. The transfer was never R-limited, so 43× was never reachable in this topology. The bottom of my band is the right answer for the wrong reason.

The defeating mechanism is fixable, and the price is exactly the exchange

If the signal wire recovers poorly because it is filled and drained through the cell's own channel resistance, then making that path adiabatic must improve it. Bookings — every idealisation, and the direction it biases the result
  • Ideal CMOS supply. Favours CMOS — a real regulator adds loss. Every ratio here is conservative for QAL.
  • Tank pre-charge not costed. Favours QAL. Every row is a bound on that account; the committed topup variant is what costs it.
  • Ideal gate-drive and VHI rails, metered but not generated. Favours QAL — CMOS needs no such rail. Measured 3.86 → 8.33 fJ of gate drive across the 4-bank run.
  • Lumped wire capacitance. Applied identically to both sides, and measured to be worth 0.01 % against a π ladder carrying the PDK's own resistance.
  • No via or jog capacitance. Understates the wire, hence understates whichever side wire capacitance favours.
  • Single-shot rails. The committed deck's rails end at 0.72–0.88 V, not 0, because the return recovers only 17–25 %. Every energy here is a first-cycle energy. It cancels in the difference and is identical on both sides, but it bounds the interpretation.
  • JUNCAP leakage. The compat shim discards ad/as/pd/ps, so the default PD = 1.00 µm clips the STI-edge term on the 1.12 µm pMOS: every implied leakage figure is a lower bound.

what phase 2 inherits

The regime AES AddRoundKey actually sits in

The wire model is settled and PDK-bracketed, and the rotate length formula is closed-form. Two things carry forward with teeth in them.

The XOR2 pitch is the operating point, not an outlier. AES AddRoundKey is 128 XOR2 cells wide, and sg13g2_xor2_1 is 3.84 µm — so Cw = 9.216 fF per bit is the realistic load, and that is precisely the row where the committed configuration stops computing (fcrit S3 27/32) and QAL loses the wire term to CMOS (0.749×). Phase 2 vehicle (a) must widen the cells, slow the ramp, or both — and the mechanism probes say how much each buys.

The adder point reframes itself. This run does not measure ripple against parallel-prefix. What it does establish is that the beat is set by the rise hop and the rise hop is set by the capacitance inside the LC loop — so a wide-and-shallow adder pays its wire in the return hop and the initiation interval rather than in the beat. That is the opposite of what the bank-profile argument predicts, and it is worth measuring directly rather than asserting.

Budget note: a 4×8 row is 93–100 s of Xyce wall and ~8 MB of .prn, and each configuration needs eight sequential zero-current probes plus one fixed-point re-run. A 128-cell single-level bank is sixteen times the cell count — probe at 8 or 16 cells and scale by measured per-cell increments instead.

Phase 1 verdict. The adiabatic mechanism does apply to the dominant capacitance — but only when that capacitance is inside the resonant loop. A rotate permutation is by construction on signal nodes, behind each driving cell's own pull-up, and there the measured saving runs from 59 % down to nothing and turns negative at the PDK's own XOR2 bit pitch. The insight survives as a design instruction, not as a free win: get the wire into the charge path, or pay for the driver that makes the path adiabatic.

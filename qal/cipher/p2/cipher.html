qal/cipher/p2 · Phase 2 · AES-128 & ChaCha20 on a QAL datapath

QAL turns wires into cells

The insight under test was that wiring does the rotate — zero gates, zero levels. Inside one level boundary that is true, and this run implements it exactly that way. Across a level boundary it is false, because an unbuffered QAL bank returns its rail charge and a value that must outlive its rail has to be re-driven. On a ChaCha20 quarter-round that costs 74 % of all cells. Measured on both datapaths: QAL wins the wire term by 2.13× and still loses the vehicle — AddRoundKey needs 68 % of its bits to flip to break even on energy, and gets about 50.

01 / mechanism

Why a level boundary costs a cell

A CMOS gate holds its output against a supply that never goes away, so a signal that must travel from level j to level k is a wire: it costs capacitance and nothing else. An unbuffered QAL bank returns its rail charge at the end of its hold window, and a cell output is held up only by its own pull-up from that rail. When the rail comes down, the value is gone.

So a signal crossing L levels costs L forwarding cells — one inverter per bit per level — and the rotate permutation the insight calls free is free only where it lands on the very next level.

This is enforced, not assumed. Chain.evaluate() refuses a netlist in which any cell reads a level older than k−1, so a vehicle that needs forwarding cannot be built without paying for it.

Kogge-Stone is uniform-depth — every prefix cell reads only the level directly above it — which is exactly the property unbuffered QAL needs. A ripple CPA is not, and its forwarding bill grows as O(W²).

03 / interconnect

The wire, per bit, from the permutation

Phase 1’s PDK-derived figures are inherited unchanged — 0.0930 / 0.15 / 0.2181 fF/µm for isolated min-width Metal2, the campaign figure, and a min-pitch bus. What Phase 2 adds is the geometry of the actual permutations, per bit rather than as a mean.

ShiftRows. With the 16 bytes in the natural 4×4 tile grid and 8 bit-slices across a tile, source byte 4c+r moves to column (c−r) mod 4, so displacement depends only on the row: 0 / 8 / 16 / 8 bit pitches for r = 0…3. Row 0 does not move and pays no wire at all — a real feature of ShiftRows that a flat mean erases.

At the sg13g2_xor2_1 pitch of 3.84 µm — the cell that must physically fit per bit — AddRoundKey’s load distribution is 32 nets at 0 fF, 64 at 4.608 fF and 32 at exactly 9.216 fF. That last figure is the one Phase 1 measured as both failing its functional criterion (27/32) and losing the wire term to CMOS (0.749×). A quarter of AddRoundKey’s permutation nets sit on it.

04b / the verdict

Activity is what decides it

A QAL beat raises and returns the whole bank rail whatever the data does, so QAL's energy is activity-independent. A CMOS transition charges only the nodes that flip, so CMOS's energy scales with activity. One ratio is therefore not a verdict — the crossover activity is.

The brief nominated depth-1, N = 128 AddRoundKey as “QAL's best possible case anywhere in cryptography.” Measured, it is the worst of the three: crossover 0.677 against the adder's 0.480.

The reason is structural. QAL pays for width every beat — it charges the whole bank whether or not the data moves — while CMOS pays only for activity. Depth costs QAL beats, but each beat at least buys computation; width costs QAL charge it cannot avoid. Wide-and-shallow, the shape the insight nominated, is the shape that suits QAL least.

QAL roughly halves the cost of the rotate wire — a signal wire, on cell output nodes.

This revises Phase 1's headline, in QAL's favour — and Phase 1 predicted it. Phase 1 concluded QAL “does essentially nothing for wire behind a driver”, measuring net/stored 1.26–2.07 and ratios 0.749–1.792× on an eight-cell bank with a 124.66 ps ramp. It also named the mechanism — “the committed cell's pull-up is too resistive for the committed ramp” — and measured that slowing the ramp to 257 ps takes net/stored to 0.721 and the ratio to 2.775×. A real 32-bit cipher bank is 20× the committed capacitance, so its ramp is 322–340 ps with no device change at all: the slow ramp is not a lever here, it is what a cipher-width bank does anyway. At that ramp this run measures 0.90× and 2.13×, in the direction and roughly the magnitude Phase 1's own probe predicted.

So the insight is partially vindicated: in wire-dominated logic the adiabatic mechanism does apply to the interconnect, and to signal wire, not merely rail wire. What it does not do is carry the vehicle — the wire term runs 2.13× in QAL's favour while the total runs 0.74–0.90× against it, because the bank charge QAL cannot avoid dwarfs the wire charge it partly recovers.

The mechanism shows up directly in the per-level recycle fractions: the wire-loaded level falls from 21.80 % to 6.27 % while its unloaded neighbours hold at 37–39 %. The wire's own charge is partly recovered, and its presence simultaneously degrades the recovery of everything else sharing that rail.

05 / discipline

Comparators, gates and bookings

Two CMOS comparators, because they answer different questions. The same-netlist twin carries the identical netlist, forwarding cells and all, on an ideal supply pinned to the QAL row’s own measured rail peak — it isolates the technology at a fixed netlist. The CMOS-native form is the same function built the way CMOS would build it, with no forwarding cells; it is what an engineer would ship, so the ratio against it is the real one, and it favours CMOS further. The gap between the two is the forwarding tax, measured.

Held-source amplitude is pinned to dV = 1.65 V on both sides; only the supply is pinned to the QAL rail peak. CMOS energy is convention-free: the supply is constant, so E = vdd·ΔQ exactly, verified against C_switched·vdd² by construction.

Activity is reported, not hidden. A QAL beat raises and returns the whole bank rail whatever the data does, so QAL’s energy is activity-independent. A CMOS transition charges only the nodes that flip, so CMOS’s energy scales with activity. The CMOS rows toggle a full operand word — activity 1.0 on both cipher vehicles — which is the worst case for CMOS and therefore conservative for QAL.

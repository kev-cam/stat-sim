## P1 — `VGH = max(1.5, dV)` (PRE-STATED in the pre-registration, not a late change)

The committed harness pins the switch gate drive *and the transfer pMOS bulk* at `VGH = 1.5 V`
(`VHI vhi 0 VGH`). At `dV = 1.65 V` the bank node starts **above** that bulk and forward-biases the
pMOS source-bulk junction, so the deck would be unphysical. The rule binds at `dV = 1.65` **only**
and is bit-identical to the committed 1.5 V everywhere else, including at `dV = 1.50`.

**This was not left as an assumption.** A control was run: `dV = 1.50` with `VGH` forced to 1.65 V
(`ctl_d150_vgh165_*`, flagged `VGH_FORCED`, excluded from every optimum).

| point | VA_open @VGH 1.50 | VA_open @VGH 1.65 | delta | VBEND | t_valid90 | gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L=15 W=30 | 0.11956 | 0.10218 | **−0.01737** | 0.896→0.913 | 95.10→92.36 | PASS→PASS |
| L=10 W=30 | 0.16328 | 0.14201 | **−0.02126** | 0.878→0.899 | 87.58→84.48 | **FAIL→PASS** |

So roughly **0.02 V of the drain improvement at dV=1.65 is the gate drive, not the swing** — and
that is the same size as the margin by which the dV=1.65 optimum `d165_L10_W30` clears the 0.1478 V
gate (it reads 0.13690). Stated plainly in the results: the dV=1.65 rows enjoy a gate-drive
advantage that is *physically unavoidable* at that swing (you must drive the switch at least to the
rail), and the identical trick is separately available in-envelope at dV=1.50. Gate-charge and
gate-drive-energy columns are **not comparable across dV** for the dV=1.65 rows.

## A1 — the committed campaign carries TWO level-time conventions under one name

Found by the instrument check, which failed on exactly one of eleven quantities.

`lsw.py` sets, in memory, `t_level_ps = max(t_hop, t_valid90)`. The committed
`RESULTS_LSWEEP.json` row for the same point carries `t_level_ps = 115.570918` and
`t_level_cons_ps = 123.443114` — i.e. `reextract.py` **redefined** `t_level` to
`max(t_hop, t_settle90)` and moved the `t_valid90` form to `t_level_cons`. Worse, the docstring in
the committed `reextract.py` states the opposite of what its code does ("`t_level_cons = max(t_hop,
t_settle90)`", while the code computes `max(t_hop, t_settle90, t_valid90)`).

The **field values are self-consistent** and match the brief's own quotation ("123.443 ps
conservative / 115.571 ps settling"); only the in-memory name and one docstring disagree.

**Amended:** this track reports both, always named explicitly, and imports
`reextract.settle_times` rather than re-implementing it. After the fix the instrument check
reproduces **all eleven** committed quantities at rel ≤ 1.4e-11 (eight of them exactly 0).

## A2 — MEASURED INSTRUMENT DEFECT: an all-zero initial state will not converge

The cell-floor deck aborted at the supply step:

```
function Gear12::rejectStep:
   Maximum number of failures at time 1e-10
*** Xyce Abort ***
```

`1e-10 s` is exactly the 100 ps step time. **Bisected:** it fails for a deck of 27 cases, for 6
cases, and for **one case alone**, at **every** voltage tried including the committed 0.6754250 V —
yet the committed 12-case `cellcmp2.cir`, re-run byte-identical in this workdir under this cache,
converges and produces its committed `.mt0`. So the cause is something the committed deck **has**
that a stripped one lacks, not anything this track added.

**Cause:** a deck of only supply-stepped "qal" cases has *every node at 0 V at t = 0* — there is no
voltage anywhere in the circuit — and the DC operating point is degenerate. The committed deck
converges only because it happens to carry three DC-biased "cmos" cases alongside its nine stepped
ones.

**Fix, verified:** one DC-biased companion case per deck is sufficient. Bisected on 1 qal + 1 cmos:

- t90 at 0.6754250 V = **137.7454 ps**, digit-identical to the committed `aL278`
- 1.2 V CMOS reference = **57.1429 ps** vs the committed `bc112` **57.1432 ps**

The companion (`zc`) is therefore added to **every** chunk, which makes that committed CMOS number
a *per-deck* instrument check. It passes on all three groups (rel 5.3e-06 / 1.1e-05 / 1.2e-16), and
the three committed floor points reproduce at rel ≤ 1.3e-07. Nothing about the measured cell or its
stimulus changed.

**This is a trap for anyone re-running the floor curve**: a QAL-only floor deck under the committed
`.OPTIONS` is not a smaller version of the committed deck, it is a non-converging one.

## A3 — two harness races that cost collection passes (no measurement affected)

1. `lsw.save_row` and `floor.py` both did read-modify-write on one shared JSON. Under the 4-way
   parallel driver the last writer won, so the first floor collection kept only one chunk's rows
   and the first grid pass would have lost rows. **Fixed:** every point/chunk writes its own file
   (`rowd/<tag>.json`, `part_floor_<chunk>.json`) and an explicit `merge` / `MERGEPARTS` step
   combines them in one process.
2. The exact-VBEND floor case list was rebuilt from `rows.json` at collection time. When round 3
   added 13 grid rows the chunk boundaries **shifted**, so a rebuilt chunk's metadata no longer
   matched the deck that had been run (it raised `ValueError: 'V(O_V06195239)' is not in list`
   rather than mis-reporting). **Fixed:** the list is frozen to `vb_cases.json` and each chunk
   writes its own results, so metadata always comes from the process that ran the deck.

Neither touched a simulated number: both were caught as a missing key or a missing group, never as
a wrong value.

## A4 — the C2 rail-drain gate is one-sided and therefore vacuous for o21ai

`C2` is `VA_open <= 0.1478 V`. Every o21ai bank row has **negative** `VA_open` (−0.113 to −0.228):
bank A gives up *more* charge than it stored, because the unsettled 2-high stack conducts DC. A
negative value passes a one-sided gate trivially, so C2-as-written cannot see this failure mode.

**Amended for the o21ai discussion only** (the inverter-bank grid is unaffected — every row there
has positive `VA_open`): the o21ai rows are judged on the two-sided quantity and on the sign, which
is reported explicitly. No inverter-bank verdict changes.

  Every time and every energy here is a **LOWER BOUND** on a real layout.
